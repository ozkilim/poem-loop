When first I saw you laughing in the rain,
Your coat undone, your hair a tangled thread,
I swore I'd never know the cold again
Nor sleep alone in any narrow bed.
You taught me how the kettle sings at six,
How bread can rise if left to trust the dark,
How every broken thing a hand can fix,
How one small light can make the whole night stark.
And if the years should thin us into frost,
And if our names grow quiet on the tongue,
I will not count a single day as lost,
For every day with you, my love, was young.
So hold my hand, the evening's coming in;
We'll let it find us, smiling, skin to skin.
