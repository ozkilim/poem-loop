Recursive poem experiment · one local model, every call fresh

The Perfect Poem Loop

A local Qwen model was asked to “write the perfect poem”. A fresh copy of the same model then critiques it, and another fresh copy rewrites it using only that critique. This repeats 100 times per chain. Where does the poem end up?

What we found

It never settles. It erodes.

None of the five chains converged, and none ever repeated a poem. Each rewrite keeps about 90% of the previous poem’s text, so every step looks like polishing. After 100 small steps only 12–28% of the original poem is left.

Rhyme dies everywhere

All five chains start as rhymed, consoling poems; the share of rhyming lines falls from 29–56% to 0–11%. Length also roughly halves, to 51–93 words, mostly in the first 20 rounds.

People disappear

In four of five chains “we”, “love”, “heart” and “hands” vanish for good: by round 21, 40, 68 and 68. Only chain 1 keeps a pair of palms, holding up a lintel.

Different words, one destination

The final poems share only about 23% of their wording, yet all five land in the same style: “The Valley’s Jaw”, “Lintel”, “The Maw”, “Asphyxiation”, “The Cold Weld”. Jaws, throats, gullets, rust, frost and stone. The model’s “perfect poem” is a style, not a text.

The critic never runs out of notes

Of 500 critiques, 437 ask to cut clichés and 379 warn about abstraction; none asks for rhyme. Every round it praises the poem, then pushes it a little harder and darker. That steady current is what the poems drift on.
